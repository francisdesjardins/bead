Bead DevTools and your data

The extension shows what a page’s Bead clients do. It sends nothing anywhere.

In short

Bead DevTools is a Chrome extension that adds a Bead panel to DevTools. The panel shows the calls, envelopes, metrics and broker parcels of the Bead clients built by the page you inspect.

  • It reads only what a page’s Bead clients tell its hook, and Bead’s own HTTP exchanges.
  • It keeps what it reads in the panel’s memory, until the panel closes or you clear it.
  • It sends nothing out of your browser. It has no server, no analytics, no ads, and it runs no code that is not in its package.
  • It asks Chrome for no permission. It does not sell or share any data.

What it reads

On every page, before the page’s own scripts run, the extension defines a hook. A Bead client built in that page announces itself to the hook. Until a Bead panel is open on the tab, the hook passes on nothing but that a client announced itself, and its kind.

While a Bead panel is open on the tab, the hook and the panel read:

  • each call the page’s Bead clients make or answer: its procedure, its timings, its status, and its payloads as text and as a tree;
  • each envelope those clients send and receive, and each client’s metrics;
  • the parcels, link changes and reports of each Bead broker participant the page builds;
  • the stack of the code that made each call, its scripts’ addresses and lines, so the panel can open it in Sources;
  • in the Network panel, only the exchanges whose content type is Bead’s, application/prs.bead, and their bodies. It skips every other exchange.

A credential a call carries is recorded by its scheme alone, never its token. A page or an application can keep its clients from the panel by giving them devtools: false.

What it keeps

What the panel reads stays in its memory. It is gone when the panel closes, when DevTools closes, or when you clear it. With Preserve log on, it stays across a reload of the page, while DevTools stays open. The extension writes none of it to your disk.

The panel keeps two choices of yours in the extension’s own storage, on your device: where it places a call’s detail, and the share of the split between the list and the detail. It keeps nothing else there.

Three actions write or read a file, and each only when you ask:

  • Export saves the records the panel holds as a JSON file, where your browser saves files.
  • Import reads a JSON file you choose, in the panel alone.
  • A call’s menu copies its request, its answer or a snippet to your clipboard.

What it sends

Nothing leaves your browser. The extension makes no network request of its own. It has no analytics, no telemetry, no crash report and no account. Its code is all in the package the Chrome Web Store distributes, and it loads no remote code.

The records travel inside the browser alone: from the hook, in the page, to the extension’s bridge, then to its service worker, then to the panel on the same tab, by Chrome’s own messaging.

The extension changes nothing on the page but the hook it defines. It reloads the page only when you ask it to.

Its permissions

The extension’s manifest names no permission and no host permission. It declares four parts:

  • two content scripts on every address, <all_urls>, at document_start: the hook, in the page’s own world, which must be in place before the page’s Bead client loads; and the bridge, in the extension’s isolated world, which carries the hook’s records to the panel;
  • a DevTools page, which adds the Bead panel to DevTools;
  • a service worker, which pairs each tab with the panel open on it.

Chrome warns that such content scripts can read and change your data on every site. The hook reads only what a page’s Bead clients give it, and keeps it within your browser.

Contact
